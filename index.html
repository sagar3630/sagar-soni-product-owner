LYC2025 to now

Love Ya Catering

An Indigenous-owned marketplace where companies order catering from local kitchens, and every order helps fund First Nations communities.

Product Owner · DianApps · Australia

  1. Context
    The founder would soon be quoting order and revenue numbers to funders and partners.
    Decision
    Google Analytics covers the marketing site and the first steps of ordering. Orders, revenue and GMV come from our own database, and the admin dashboard isn't tracked by Google at all.
    Why
    Google Analytics and the finance books never agree. Two sources for the same number lose trust faster than having no dashboard.
  2. Context
    Feedback from the first restaurants showed that one platform-wide default, a fixed 15 km radius for everyone, didn't suit small kitchens or bigger caterers.
    Decision
    Move both settings into each restaurant's profile, with an admin override per restaurant.
    Why
    Partners stay in control of what they can deliver, which means fewer declined and late orders.
  3. Context
    Feedback was coming in from the client, internal reviews and partner testing all at once.
    Decision
    One feedback register. Each item is rewritten as a clear issue and recommendation and ranked High (before launch), Medium (next sprint) or Low (backlog).
    Why
    The team always knew what blocked launch and what could wait.

TG2025 to now

Thousand Greens

A members-only network where private-club golfers host each other at their home courses, across 2,300+ clubs in 40+ countries. Hosting is voluntary, so the network depends on the people who give more than they take.

Product Owner · DianApps · Global, mostly US

  1. Context
    New members were churning after a few cold requests that nobody answered.
    Decision
    Replace raw club percentages with a High, Medium or Low acceptance rating on the map and club page, and default new members to High and Medium until their first games.
    Why
    An early yes is what keeps a new member, and the data to predict it already existed.
  2. Context
    A dashboard showed active clubs falling, and it looked like a supply crisis.
    Decision
    Look at the full game history before reacting. The drop was a strong seasonal pattern in colder regions, and underneath it supply was growing.
    Why
    It pointed effort at the real problem: member behaviour at high-demand clubs.
  3. Context
    Features can match their spec exactly and still miss their goal.
    Decision
    System reviews check what changed, the rule behind it, and whether the outcome it was built for is actually happening in production.
    Why
    It catches design misses and finished-but-not-live gaps that bug hunts don't.

DS2026 to now

Divya Santan

A consultation app that helps expecting parents find, book and talk to trusted consultants through their pregnancy. The client brought a prototype; the job was to turn it into a buildable MVP.

Product Owner · DianApps · India

  1. Context
    The prototype had no clear line between what a consultant earns and what a parent pays.
    Decision
    Consultants enter their fee at onboarding. Admin sets the display price, the fee plus the platform margin, during review. The fee is locked once approved.
    Why
    Consultants see one predictable number, and the business controls margin and discounts in one place.
  2. Context
    Care plans run over many sessions, and the fit with a consultant doesn't always work.
    Decision
    Allow one change per part of the plan while at least one session is still unbooked, and handle any fee difference through admin.
    Why
    Parents keep their plan instead of abandoning it, and the rule stays simple enough to build and support.
  3. Context
    Rescheduling was first parked as out of scope.
    Decision
    Bring it into the MVP for both parents and consultants, with clear notice windows.
    Why
    Without it, every change in a pregnancy schedule becomes a support ticket.

TV2023 to 2025

TaxVero

Tax resolution software that turns raw IRS transcripts into a case analysis, a resolution path and pre-filled forms for tax professionals.

BA, then Product Owner · Nablasol · US tax practices

  1. Context
    MyCase.Tax read like a case tracker, but the product had grown into transcripts, analysis, forms and a client portal.
    Decision
    Run a full rebrand: name, logo and website, with a redirect so existing users weren't lost.
    Why
    Positioning has to match what the product actually does for practitioners today.
  2. Context
    Transcript codes, multi-year balances and collection deadlines are full of exceptions.
    Decision
    Each story lists its exception cases as acceptance criteria.
    Why
    Fewer surprises in review, and fewer escaped defects in a compliance-heavy product.

How I work

Data before the PRD.

I read the requests, orders or games first, find where the core loop breaks, and only then write the brief.

Edge cases are acceptance criteria.

Exceptions go into the story, not into a bug ticket after release, so QA and developers share one definition of done.

One source for every number.

Each metric has a single owner and source, so the founder, finance and the dashboard always agree.

I run several client products at once with a team of 15+ across design, development and QA. An AI workflow I built on Claude Code and Jira cuts story definition and edge-case mapping from 30 minutes to under 10. At Nablasol, the 12-person team I ran ceremonies for reached 90% sprint goal completion over 4+ release cycles.
