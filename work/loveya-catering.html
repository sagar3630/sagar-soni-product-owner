An Indigenous-owned marketplace where companies order catering from local kitchens, and every order helps fund First Nations communities.

Project overview

Office managers and procurement teams order catering for meetings and events. Partner restaurants accept and fulfil orders from their own app. An admin team approves partners, sets commission and handles refunds and payouts. The business was preparing for a beta, a controlled launch and a public launch at a national Indigenous business event.

Customer web and app, restaurant app, admin. Built on Next.js, React Native and Django.

Calls I made

Count money from our own records, not Google

Context
The founder was about to share order and sales numbers with funders and partners.
Decision
Use Google Analytics only to count website visits. Take order and sales numbers from our own database.
Why
Google's numbers never match the accounts. If two places show different totals, nobody trusts either.

Let each restaurant set its own time and distance limits

Context
One rule for every restaurant didn't work. Small kitchens and big caterers needed different limits.
Decision
Each restaurant sets how long it has to accept an order and how far it delivers. Admin can change it if needed.
Why
Restaurants only take orders they can handle, so fewer orders get turned down or arrive late.

Fix what blocks the launch first

Context
Feedback was coming from the client, our own reviews and restaurant testing, all at once.
Decision
Put it all in one list, write each item clearly, and rank it: fix before launch, fix next sprint, or fix later.
Why
Everyone knew what had to be fixed before launch and what could wait.

Track 53 things at launch, not 129

Context
The first tracking plan recorded almost every tap in all three apps, and another team had to build it.
Decision
Launch with the 53 events that answer real questions. Move 55 to later and drop 21 we didn't need.
Why
A short list gets built correctly, and every launch number comes from one clear source.
