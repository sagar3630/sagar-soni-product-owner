The product

Office managers and procurement teams order catering for meetings and events. Partner restaurants accept and fulfil orders from their own app. An admin team approves partners, sets commission and handles refunds and payouts. The business was preparing for a beta, a controlled launch and a public launch at a national Indigenous business event.

Customer web and app, restaurant app, admin. Built on Next.js, React Native and Django.

What I did

  • Owned product decisions end to endTurned the founder's business goals into ordering workflows and the transactional core: cart, checkout, order acceptance, refunds and payouts.
  • Ran the go-liveA launch checklist across five areas (infrastructure, security, payments, store submissions and support), plus the App Store and Play Store submissions.
  • Handled change requestsFor example, letting each restaurant set its own delivery radius, which had been a fixed 15 km for everyone.
  • Designed the analytics planA Firebase event plan for the apps, and a Google Analytics review that kept GA to the top of the funnel and left revenue figures to the database.
  • Planned finance reconciliationBetween Stripe and Xero, so payouts, refunds and fees match the books.
  • Sped up merchant onboardingA bulk menu-upload template and clearer sign-up steps for restaurants.

Calls I made

Keep analytics honest: GA for traffic, the database for revenue

Context

The founder would soon be quoting order and revenue numbers to funders and partners.

Decision

Use Google Analytics only on the marketing site and the first part of the order funnel. Orders, revenue and GMV come from our own database, and the admin dashboard isn't tracked by Google at all.

Why

GA and the finance books never reconcile. Two sources for the same number lose trust faster than having no dashboard.

Restaurants set their own acceptance window and delivery radius

Context

Feedback from the first restaurants showed that one platform-wide default didn't suit small kitchens or bigger caterers.

Decision

Move both settings into each restaurant's profile, with an admin override per restaurant.

Why

Partners stay in control of what they can deliver, which means fewer declined and late orders.

Triage feedback by launch risk, not by who raised it

Context

Feedback was coming in from the client, internal reviews and partner testing all at once.

Decision

One feedback register, with each item rewritten as a clear issue and recommendation and ranked High (before launch), Medium (next sprint) or Low (backlog).

Why

The team always knew what blocked launch and what could wait.
