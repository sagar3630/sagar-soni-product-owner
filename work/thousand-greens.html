Project overview

A member asks to play at another club, members of that club choose whether to host, and the two play together. Hosting is voluntary, so the network depends on the people who give more than they take. Most of my work was about finding and rewarding those people.

iOS, Android, web and admin, on an annual membership model.

What I did

  • Read the data before writing the PRDAnalysed requests, games and memberships to find where the core loop breaks, then turned each gap into a feature brief.
  • Took the acceptance-probability rating liveA High, Medium or Low rating on the map and club page shows members how likely a club is to say yes before they ask.
  • Co-designed and delivered membership pricingOne reciprocity rule that rewards hosting replaced a confusing set of tiers, so members who host more than they play renew free. Plus a pause option at half dues for a season off.
  • Fixed referral attributionMatching a friend's email at signup, plus a backfill of credits already missed, so members get credit when someone they brought in joins.
  • Overhauled notificationsOne rules engine, topic and channel preferences, and fewer muting dead ends.
  • Shipped engagement featuresA redesigned home screen, onboarding fixes, referral credit matching and charity donations at the end of a game.
  • Set up AI-assisted QAA QA process that links the production database, the code repo and the PRDs. I ran it alone for about two months, handed it to a QA engineer, and the client doubled QA time from 4 to 8 hours a day.
  • Built read-only analytics dashboardsOn production data, for user behaviour and demographic insights that feed roadmap planning. They weren't in the client's requirements.
  • Kept the spec true to productionChecked every shipped ticket against the code, not just the PRD, before updating the functional document.

Calls I made

Show new members their chances before they ask

Context
New members were giving up after sending requests that nobody answered.
Decision
Show each club as a High, Medium or Low chance of a yes, and point new members to High and Medium clubs until they've played a few games.
Why
An early yes keeps a new member, and we already had the data to predict it.

Compare with last year, not last month

Context
A chart showed fewer active clubs, and it looked like a crisis.
Decision
Check the full history first. The dip happened every winter in colder places, and overall the number was growing.
Why
The team didn't waste time on a problem that wasn't real, and focused on the one that was: members at popular clubs not hosting.

Check each feature does its job, not just that it works

Context
A feature can work exactly as written and still not get the result it was built for.
Decision
In every review, check what changed, the rule behind it, and whether the result is really happening for users.
Why
It catches features that work but miss the point, and fixes that are finished but not live yet.

Give referral credit however the friend joins

Context
Members only got credit if their friend used the invite link. Friends who just typed the member's name got them nothing.
Decision
Match the friend's email when they sign up, and give back the credit members had already missed.
Why
Referral credit rewards members who bring people in, so it should work however they join.
