The product

A member asks to play at another club, members of that club choose whether to host, and the two play together. Hosting is voluntary, so the network depends on the people who give more than they take. Most of my work was about finding and rewarding those people.

iOS, Android, web and admin, on an annual membership model.

What I did

  • Read the data before writing the PRDAnalysed requests, games and memberships to find where the core loop breaks, then turned each gap into a feature brief.
  • Reworked request routingAn acceptance-probability score shows members how likely a club is to say yes before they ask, and sends requests to the right hosts first.
  • Rebuilt membership pricingOne reciprocity rule that rewards hosting replaced a confusing set of tiers, plus a pause option at half dues for a season off.
  • Overhauled notificationsOne rules engine, topic and channel preferences, and fewer muting dead ends.
  • Shipped engagement featuresA redesigned home screen, onboarding fixes, referral credit matching and charity donations at the end of a game.
  • Kept the spec true to productionChecked every shipped ticket against the code, not just the PRD, before updating the functional document.

Calls I made

Show new members their odds before they send a request

Context

New members were churning after a few cold requests that nobody answered.

Decision

Replace raw club percentages with a High, Medium or Low acceptance rating on the map and club page, and default new members to High and Medium until their first games.

Why

An early yes is what keeps a new member, and the data to predict it already existed.

Read supply year over year, not month over month

Context

A dashboard showed active clubs falling, and it looked like a supply crisis.

Decision

Look at the full game history before reacting. The drop was a strong seasonal pattern in colder regions, and underneath it supply was growing.

Why

It stopped the team from fixing a problem that didn't exist and pointed effort at the real one: member behaviour at high-demand clubs.

Judge every feature by its intended outcome, not just whether it works

Context

Features can match their spec exactly and still miss their goal.

Decision

System reviews check what changed, the rule behind it, and whether the outcome it was built for is actually happening in production.

Why

It catches design misses and dev-complete-but-not-live gaps that bug hunts don't.
